## Data Quality agent

From `reports/eda/DATA_QUALITY.md` (read-only EDA, `scripts/eda.py`) and the rules in `docs/DATA_POLICY.md` (`echos/agents/data_quality.py`, counts in `reports/metrics/data_quality.json`).

- 56 patients, median window 54 days; no BP/HR reading on 39.9% of patient-days (1152 of 2885); 18 patients have a gap > 7 days (max 91).
- Activity outages: 128 zero-event and 56 low-event (< 5% of the patient's median) patient-days; 2 system-wide days (2019-06-15, 06-16), 99 patient-specific.
- Implausible physiology values: 9 of 17679 (0.05%). Exact duplicates and out-of-order rows: 0 in Physiology, Activity and Labels.
- Labels: 608 rows, 49 patients, 7 patients with none. Base rates: agitation 4.19%, vitals alert 18.2%.
- Policy: out-of-range values become NaN and are listed (never dropped silently, never imputed); no forward-fill of vitals; sensor-gap days have NaN behaviour features instead of "zero activity" and are excluded from baselines; a day with a sensor gap, unchecked data, or more than 7 days without vitals is DATA_GAP and cannot be Normal (Urgent still wins). Daily reports carry a data note.

## Report agent: RAG, LLM and privacy

- **RAG:** 5 guideline PDFs (CDC STEADI, NICE NG249, NG97, NG136, RCP NEWS2) -> 306 chunks (~250 words, 50 overlap), local `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` embeddings, Chroma in `data/rag/`; top-3 chunks per finding type with ids `[KB-n]`. Top-1 relevance was eyeballed with `scripts/rag_check.py` (cosine 0.54-0.71 for 10 typical findings, sources matched the topic; critical blood pressure returned a NEWS2 chunk and the NEWS2 PDF's metadata title is just "News Report"). Retrieval quality has not been measured against labelled relevance.
- **Guardrails:** paraphrase only, every guidance statement cites a retrieved id, validator rejects unknown/missing citations, copied runs of 10+ words and diagnostic wording; template fallback prints source names; weekly report lists references for clinician review. `rag.enabled: false` turns it off.
- **LLM:** Gemini via `google-genai` through a budget manager (response cache, primary model, fallback model, template); limiter 20% below each model's per-minute limit (`llm.rpm`), HTTP 429 = 60 s cooldown and move on without retry, timeouts never retried, backlog 200 (see `config.yaml`). The LLM is not on the alert path.
- **Privacy:** only structured summaries (pseudonymous patient id, day, flags, statistics, evidence ids) and retrieved public guideline text are sent to the LLM; no raw sensor rows. Embedding is local. The LLM never decides emergencies and alerts are written before report generation.

## Reading the tables

- **The vitals sub-model** beats the fixed BP rule on PR-AUC (0.70 vs 0.26; CIs do not overlap). At the rule's own alert count it recalls 0.89 vs 0.76, though the CIs overlap. At ~1 alert/patient-week it recalls 0.71 with 0.40 false alerts/patient-week. The target includes `water_alert`, which has no BP/HR signal, and labels are likely threshold-derived, so part of this is re-learning the labelling rule.
- **The behaviour sub-model (headline = within-patient):** median per-patient ROC-AUC 0.818 (IQR 0.655-0.976), PR-AUC 0.449 (IQR 0.146-0.584) over 27 patients with both classes; the pooled numbers (ROC 0.86, PR-AUC 4.4x base rate) are inflated by between-patient activity-level differences. Without absolute levels, pooled ROC falls to 0.66 but within-patient ROC stays ~0.83, so per-patient z-scores carry the within-patient signal. 27 patients only; IQRs are wide. The pooled score has real but modest skill (PR-AUC 4.4x the base rate, ROC-AUC 0.86); most of that comes from same-day raw activity level. The personal-baseline drift score alone is at chance (ROC 0.45), so "routine drift" is not evidence of agitation here.
- **The fall agent** is near-ceiling on adults in SisFall (tables above and the fall audit section). Elderly sensitivity rests on a single subject (SE06) and is much lower once post-fall inactivity is required. False alarms per hour are measured on short vigorous SisFall ADL trials and overstate daily life, but are still high for continuous wear; the triage rules therefore require post-fall inactivity for URGENT (operating point in `config.yaml`, chosen by `scripts/fall_audit.py`).
<!-- reading_e2e_fault:start -->
- **End-to-end (template mode, DryRun mail, out-of-fold vitals and behaviour scores):** 51/60 injected held-out-subject falls raised an URGENT alert (85.0%, Wilson 95% CI 0.739-0.919); 2 false fall alerts (0.011 per patient-week). Alerts are decided at day close, so the simulated delay is 13.1 h median (max 23.8 h): the orchestrator is a daily batch, not real-time; a fall injected through the live controller (which closes the day at once) is queued in about 0.31 s on average. 28.8% of patient-days are Monitor (4.5% carry a data gap), above the 25% I consider usable for caregivers; switching the vitals trigger off would leave 15.3%, the agitation trigger 21.5% (see the MONITOR driver section; a threshold change is proposed, not applied).
- **Fault injection (fakes, 3 patients x 10 days, compared with a clean run):** no alert lost and no duplicate in 11 of 11 fault cases; the exception is the SMTP outage longer than the retry window, where alerts were queued but 10 outbox rows ended FAILED (undelivered, visible; measured before the 15-minute retry window and 2-hour sweeper were added, see Limitations). Worker kills were detected in 2.9-3.1 s and recovered (first new output) in 3.2-11.1 s; a fall injected during such an outage is delayed by up to the restart time but not lost. Duplicates are impossible by construction (unique alert key). Differences from the clean run were restricted to quality / degraded flags.

- **Bugs found and fixed while producing these results:** (1) `run_orchestrator` could stop before processing days inserted during a slow pass (lost alerts under a slow LLM); (2) the vitals sub-model's bootstrap CIs for recall at an alert budget undercounted patient-weeks for resampled duplicate patients, biasing them low. Point estimates are unchanged.

## Limitations

- **Single small cohort:** ~49-54 labelled TIHM patients from one study over 3 months (2019-04-01 to 2019-06-30); agitation occurs in only 27 patients. Confidence intervals are wide and a patient bootstrap only partly reflects that. Nothing here has been tested on another cohort or setting.
- **Labels are clinical alerts**, not confirmed health events; they probably derive from thresholds on the same measurements. The vitals model's skill may reflect recovering that rule. The behaviour model detects same-day agitation labels; it does not forecast.
- **Falls are simulated.** TIHM has no falls and no accelerometer: falls are SisFall windows (young volunteers, scripted falls on mats) injected into TIHM patient-days. Fall results say nothing about real home falls, and the end-to-end detection rate is a pipeline check on held-out SisFall subjects (SA03, SA11, SA19, SE06).
- **Elderly fall evidence is one subject:** only SE06 (a judo expert) performed falls; every elderly cell in the per-type table has 5 trials of one person, and the confidence interval of the elderly sensitivity is degenerate. Several fall types are weak or missed (see the per-type table); elderly sensitivity cannot be generalised.
- **The agitation score has modest skill and is not a probability:** it beats the base rate (behaviour table) but much of that comes from between-patient differences in activity level, absolute precision is low, and personal-baseline drift alone is at chance. It is shown only as a Monitor flag, never as a percentage to caregivers, clinicians or the LLM.
- **Replay numbers:** vitals and behaviour scores are out-of-fold, but the MONITOR thresholds were chosen on those same scores (mild optimism); injected sensor windows are unrelated to the patient's TIHM data; the MONITOR share is above the 25% I consider usable and a threshold change is proposed, not applied (MONITOR driver section).
- **Alerts depend on SMTP delivery.** Alerts are always queued in the outbox (none lost in the fault tests), but delivery depends on the mail server: a FALL / CRITICAL_VITALS e-mail is retried with backoff for 15 minutes (outbox PENDING -> RETRYING -> SENT or FAILED) and a sweeper retries FAILED rows every 5 minutes for 2 hours; after that a row stays FAILED until retried by hand (`POST /api/outbox/{id}/retry`). The SMTP-outage fault case above was measured before this retry window existed (3 attempts, then FAILED); the new behaviour is covered by `tests/test_email_retry.py` with a fake clock and has not been re-measured by fault injection. Real SMTP is never exercised by tests or demos (FakeMailer / DryRun only). A fall alert raised while an agent or the supervisor is down is delayed by up to the restart time (seconds for a worker; the operator's restart time for the supervisor) but never dropped.
- **Fault injection is small:** 3 patients x 10 days, one fault per case, one machine, fakes for the LLM, SMTP and PubMed; it does not cover disk failure, host loss, real network faults or the behaviour of the real Gemini API.
- **LLM wording quality was not evaluated.** All tables used template mode or fakes; the RAG guidance path (paraphrase / citation checks) is covered by unit tests and fakes, not by a study of real model output.
- **Decision support, not diagnosis.** Triage is a fixed if-then rule set; the LLM never decides emergencies. Nothing here has been clinically validated.


**LLM guardrails (report generation):** per-call timeout (15 s when those runs were made; `config.yaml` now has 45 s) (timeouts are never retried, other failures retry at most once), a requests/min limiter (20 then; now 8), and LLM wording is produced in a background worker after the template report and the alert are already written, so it cannot block alerts or the next case. Every fallback is logged with a reason (`data/llm_fallbacks.log` and the report's validation record). Real-client test with the timeout forced to 1 s (the Gemini API itself rejects HTTP deadlines under 10 s, so sub-10 s timeouts are enforced by a watchdog thread): 0 lost alerts, 0 extra, 45/45 cases identical to the clean run, 47/51 reports fell back to template (42 timeouts, 5 API client errors), 4 completed in under 1 s. In the other real-LLM runs 3-4 of 51 reports failed validation and 5-6 hit an API client error (cause not diagnosed; possibly quota).

## Fall window audit (written by scripts/fall_audit.py)

v1 fall windows were centred exactly on the max-magnitude sample while ADL windows were sliding windows, so the peak's position alone separated the classes (AUC of |peak offset from window centre|: v1 0.999, v2 0.501). v2 builds falls and ADL peak windows identically (own max sample, same length, +/- 0.5 s jitter) and keeps sliding ADL windows for false-alarm exposure. Random forest, threshold 0.5, identical subject-level folds:

| Measure | v1 (old) | v2 (new) |
|---|---|---|
| PR-AUC (falls vs ADL windows as built) | 0.995 | 0.997 |
| PR-AUC (falls vs sliding ADL) | 0.995 | 0.994 |
| Adult sensitivity | 0.982 | 0.979 |
| Adult specificity (ADL windows as built) | 0.999 | 0.989 |
| Adult specificity (sliding windows) | 0.999 | 0.999 |
| Elderly sensitivity (one subject, SE06) | 0.867 | 0.813 |
| Adult false alarms / hour | 2.317 | 2.633 |

Adult threshold curve, v2 windows (sensitivity / false alarms per hour):

| Threshold | No confirmation | With post-fall inactivity |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.979 / 2.63 | 0.943 / 1.37 |
| 0.6 | 0.972 / 1.90 | 0.937 / 0.95 |
| 0.7 | 0.965 / 1.58 | 0.930 / 0.74 |
| 0.8 | 0.952 / 1.16 | 0.918 / 0.42 |
| 0.9 | 0.907 / 0.74 | 0.873 / 0.32 |

Chosen operating point: threshold 0.8, inactivity confirmation required (adult sensitivity 0.918, 0.42 false alarms/h). Rule: 0.5 false alarms/h or fewer with sensitivity >= 0.9 if possible, otherwise the inactivity-confirmed setting. tradeoff: no unconfirmed threshold has FA/h <= 0.5 with sensitivity >= 0.9 (best unconfirmed: none under the FA limit); inactivity-confirmed setting chosen.

SisFall ADL trials are short, vigorous trials (running, jumping, stairs, sit-downs), so alarms per hour overstate daily life; SisFall falls are scripted on mats by young volunteers, and only one elderly subject performed falls.
