# Limitations

ECHOS is decision support, not diagnosis. Nothing here has been validated clinically. Numbers are from `reports/metrics/*.json` and `reports/RESULTS.md`; confidence intervals are wide.

## Data and labels
- **Small, single cohort.** About 49 labelled TIHM patients (56 with data) from one study over 3 months (2019-04-01 to 2019-06-30). Nothing was tested on another cohort or setting, and a patient bootstrap only partly reflects the uncertainty.
- **TIHM labels: provenance not verified by us.** The dataset papers describe the labels as clinician-verified events at 6-hour resolution; we did not verify how each label was produced. 99% of BP-label days have a BP reading, so the vitals model may partly learn the link between labelled events and readings: treat vitals results as an upper bound on generalisation. The target also includes body-water labels that have no BP/HR signal. Our label counts match the dataset description (49 patients have any label). Agitation labels (27 of 56 patients) are present in Labels.csv but not used: there is no behaviour detector.
- **Vitals arrive about every other day.** There is no BP/HR reading on 37.3% of patient-days (1030 of 2763, counted from each patient's first to last day with a record; `reports/eda/DATA_QUALITY.md`), and 18 patients have a gap longer than 7 days. A change can therefore be seen days late, and a day without a reading is reported as such rather than imputed. ECHOS does not use the room sensors (Activity.csv), so it cannot tell a silent home from a quiet one.

## Falls
- **Falls are simulated.** TIHM has no falls and no accelerometer. Falls are SisFall windows (scripted, on mats, mostly young volunteers) injected into TIHM patient-days, so the detection rate is a pipeline check on 4 held-out subjects (SA03, SA11, SA19, SE06), not clinical sensitivity.
- **Elderly fall evidence is one subject.** Only SE06 performed falls (75 trials, 5 per type). At the chosen operating point sensitivity is 0.56 (42/75); for that subject fall types F10 and F13 were never detected (0/5) and F07 once (1/5). For adults the weaker types are backward falls: F14 0.82, F11 0.83 and F02 0.88 (all fall types in `reports/RESULTS.md`). The elderly result cannot be generalised, and its confidence interval is degenerate.
- **A stricter operating point cut false alerts and cost detection.** Injected-fall detection fell from 0.95 (57/60, earlier windows and operating point) to 0.85 (51/60) once the unbiased v2 windows and the post-fall-inactivity requirement were used; the current replay detects 0.77 (46/60; Wilson 95% CI 0.646-0.856). The later drop from 51 to 46 came with changes of the replayed day set (the behaviour detector, then the room-sensor stream, were removed; the injected falls are sampled over the replayed days), not with a change of the fall detector; the CIs overlap widely. Replay false fall alerts are 3 (0.016 per patient-week; 19 days, 0.105, before the change of operating point) and SisFall sliding-window false alarms fell from 0.76 to 0.28 per hour. Without inactivity a flagged fall is only a MONITOR day ("possible fall, no inactivity"). SisFall ADL trials are short and vigorous, so false alarms per hour overstate daily life.

## Models
- **Thresholds were chosen and scored on the same out-of-fold predictions** (MONITOR alert budgets, fall operating point), so the replay results are mildly optimistic.
- **The MONITOR share is 17.1%** of 1298 replayed patient-days (20 patients), below the 25% limit set for a usable caregiver load, but this does not hold on all patients. By reason in replay: vitals only 194 of 222 Monitor days, possible fall without inactivity only 18, data gap only 6, mixed reasons 4. (Removing the behaviour detector and the room-sensor sensor-gap rule lowered the replay share from 28.8%; behaviour-only and sensor-gap-only days had made up much of it.) `scripts/evaluation/threshold_holdout.py` (thresholds chosen on 4/5 of the patients, scored on the other 1/5, `reports/metrics/threshold_holdout.json`) scores all 56 patients out-of-fold: at the shipped `triage.vitals_prob` 0.284 the MONITOR share is 27.8% of days (held-out mean 27.7%), from a vitals flag on 14.2% of days and a data gap alone (no vitals reading for more than 7 days) on 13.5%. Choosing the vitals threshold to reach 25% on training patients gives 0.25-0.51 per fold, keeps about 0.63 of the vitals recall instead of 0.70 and still misses the cap on held-out patients in 14 of 25 folds, so a higher threshold is **not applied**. What was changed instead is how the load is shown: a day whose only reasons are data problems (`attention_kind: data`) is listed on *Today* under "Data needs checking" (a device, not the person), separate from "Changed from their usual". Triage itself is unchanged: such a day is still never NORMAL.

## Timing and reliability
- **Replay fall delay is a batch artefact.** The orchestrator decides once per patient-day, so the simulated delay is 13.2 h median (23.8 h max). Through the live controller an injected fall is queued in well under a second (0.29 s median wall clock in the fake-mailer run).
- **During an agent outage a fall alert is delayed, not lost.** In the fault runs a killed worker delayed the injected fall by about 3-5 s (restart time); with the supervisor also dead and restarted after 10 s the delay was about 11 s. No alert was lost or duplicated.
- **Restarts are layered, and the top layer is a single process.** The supervisor restarts dead detector workers (replay mode); the watchdog (`echos/orchestrator/watchdog.py`, started by `scripts/run_api.sh`) restarts the runtime process that holds the supervisor, the live processor, the Care Coordinator and the emulator when it exits or stops reporting (unit-tested with throw-away child processes; backoff 1 to 30 s). If the watchdog itself dies nothing restarts it unless an OS service manager does: ready-made launchd / systemd templates are in `scripts/service/` (not installed by anything, not tested on a real host). In live mode a restart loses nothing: the processor continues from its checkpoint and rebuilds the open day from `event_log`, the emulator resumes paused at its position.
- **Alerts depend on SMTP.** Delivery retries with backoff for 15 minutes and a sweeper retries FAILED rows every 5 minutes for 2 hours; after that a FAILED row stays visible and can be retried by hand (`POST /api/outbox/{id}/retry`). In the SMTP-outage fault case 10 outbox rows ended FAILED; that case was measured before the 15-minute window and sweeper were added and has not been re-measured (the new retry logic is covered by unit tests with a fake clock). Real SMTP is never exercised by tests or demos (fake and dry-run mailers only).
- **Fault injection is small:** 3 patients x 10 days, one fault per case, one machine, fakes for the LLM, SMTP and PubMed; no disk failure, host loss or real network faults.

## Reports and language models
- **The real LLM path is only checked by opt-in live scripts** (`scripts/realservices/live_llm_check.py`, `scripts/realservices/live_report_check.py`, each needing `--yes` and a hard request cap). All tables and tests use template mode or fakes, so the quality of real model wording was not evaluated, and the validator catches the rules it encodes, not every unsafe sentence.
- **PubMed abstracts are not shown.** They are used only to check that a source matches its finding; reports list title, journal, year and link, and the weekly report keeps at most one PubMed article per finding. Where nothing relevant is retrieved the report says "no supporting source retrieved".
- **Report clarity and usefulness were not measured with readers.** A rating kit (`scripts/docgen/make_rating_sheet.py`) exists; it has not been run and is not planned. `scripts/evaluation/report_text_check.py` (`reports/metrics/report_text_check.json`) is an automated **proxy**, not a substitute: it scores the stored template daily reports for reading level (own syllable heuristic), length, named next steps, banned or jargon wording and leftover template syntax. It passes simple, fixed template text, so a pass shows the wording rules hold, not that a reader understands or acts on a report; it does not cover LLM-written text, and its thresholds are conventional plain-language targets we chose before running it.

## Live / emulator mode
- **There is no real device.** Live mode accepts events through `POST /api/ingest/events` from any source, but the only source shipped is a labelled emulator that replays real TIHM raw events (room sensor, vitals) in wall-clock time and injects SisFall falls on demand. The models were trained on all TIHM patients, so an emulated run is a systems test, not accuracy evidence (same caveat as replay). `scripts/evaluation/live_parity.py` (`reports/metrics/live_parity.json`) checks that scoring from raw events equals scoring from the pre-built table: vitals identical on 52 compared patient-days.
- **Vitals are scored when a reading arrives; the day is final only when it closes.** An open day is PROVISIONAL (`case_state.provisional`). ECHOS does not watch room sensors (the ingestion API rejects `activity` events), so a home whose sensors go quiet is not noticed; a device that stops sending shows as stale in the web app (wall-clock freshness) and, for vitals, as days without a reading (DATA_GAP after more than 7 days).
- **The patient's clock is the newest data time seen**, so a source that stops sending everything looks "stale" in the UI (wall-clock freshness) but the day does not advance on its own. Late events for a day that has already closed are stored and ignored by the decision.
- **A fall window arrives as one burst** (impact window plus the seconds after it), as a device-side trigger would send it; there is no continuous accelerometer stream and no on-device detection.
- **Live fault tests are small and use fakes:** duplicates, late events, a failing vitals detector, an unanalysable fall window (shown as a data gap, never dropped), SMTP down everywhere, a failing report agent and a burst of four patients (`scripts/evaluation/live_fault_check.py`, `reports/metrics/live_faults.json`; software latency from event written to alert queued about 0.15 to 0.19 s in one process, not a delivery time). No disk failure, host loss or real network faults; the emulated event rate is about 1000 per second, far above a real home.
- **Raw events are kept forever unless archived by hand.** `scripts/dataprep/archive_events.py` (dry run by default) moves old closed raw events to a compressed file; it is not scheduled, the archive is not encrypted and has no restore command, and archived events are no longer deduplicated (see docs/DATA_POLICY.md).
- **Weekly reports in live mode** are built in the background when a Sunday closes (template wording; the LLM narrative is off in simulation by config).

## Agents and the alert lifecycle
- **The Case Investigator and the Care Coordinator are rule-driven agents, not LLM agents.** They plan and act from fixed policies (bounded tool loop, timeouts, thresholds in `config.yaml`); their value is the explicit goal, trace and hand-over, not learned behaviour. The evidence level (STRONG / MODERATE / LIMITED) is a rule summary and not a probability; its usefulness to readers was not measured.
- **Escalation times are wall-clock seconds** (fall 300 s, critical vitals 1800 s) and go to ONE second contact (`ECHOS_ESCALATION_EMAIL`); there is no on-call rota, no phone call or SMS, and a missing address makes the escalation row FAIL visibly. An unacknowledged alert older than `coordinator.stale_after_s` (6 h) is marked EXPIRED, never escalated, so a restart cannot page anyone about history. Acknowledgement is a button in the web app or, when `ECHOS_LINK_SECRET` and `ECHOS_PUBLIC_URL` are set, a signed expiring link in the alert e-mail (`GET /api/ack/{token}`, no login; it acknowledges only). The link is a GET with a side effect, so a mail scanner that prefetches links could acknowledge an alert without a person having seen it; the lifecycle still records `ack_by` as `email link`. The token carries the alert id (which contains the de-identified patient id and day) in base64, not encrypted; it is valid for `alerts.ack_link_ttl_s` (24 h) and every use before then works. Mail is plain SMTP and may be read in transit. Without the two variables the mail has no link. Authentication is optional (see Scope).
- **The Investigator's computed checks were measured, and most did not help.** `scripts/evaluation/investigator_eval.py` (`reports/metrics/investigator_eval.json`) takes 517 reading-days outside the person's own usual on a vital (49 patients; same-day event rate 0.234) and compares event rates with and without each signal, with a patient bootstrap: a confirmed trend 0.237 vs 0.232 (does not separate, CI includes 0), earlier deviation days of the same person that carried an event 0.404 vs 0.199 (difference +0.21, CI +0.05 to +0.32: separates). Only that last signal may raise the evidence level (LIMITED to MODERATE); trend and an event-free history are listed as context and never change it. The labels' provenance is unverified, the separating signal's CI starts close to zero, and none of this is clinical validation.
- **The Investigator's base level rules no longer separate event days from non-event days.** On 202 replayed non-Normal non-fall days (18 patients, out-of-fold detector scores) the recorded-event rate was 0.75 for STRONG (only 4 days), 0.59 for MODERATE (105) and 0.51 for LIMITED (93); STRONG minus LIMITED +0.24 (patient-bootstrap CI -0.07 to +0.60), MODERATE minus LIMITED +0.09 (CI -0.06 to +0.24): the order is as intended but both CIs include 0, so the levels are not supported as a ranking (they were, +0.39 and +0.24, before the behaviour detector and the room-sensor gap rule were removed; most of that earlier separation came from sensor-gap days, which have no reading and so no label). A repeat day (+0.09, CI -0.00 to +0.21) and persistent vitals (+0.06, CI -0.14 to +0.22) do not separate either. The data-caveat rule now fires on 6 days only (quality unchecked or monitoring degraded). The outcome is the same label family the detectors were trained on (out-of-fold, but not independent), only 18 patients were replayed, and the level is a rule summary, not a probability (`scripts/evaluation/investigator_eval.py`, `reports/metrics/investigator_eval.json`).
- **A failed Investigator does not hide a day.** `run_investigation` (echos/team/investigator.py) logs the failure, writes no case file and still hands the Care Coordinator a `case_file_ready` marked `investigator_failed`, so the clinician-review count includes the day; the report is written without a case file.
- **Real e-mail is never exercised by tests or demos** (dry-run and fake mailers only).

## Scope
- **Authentication is optional and minimal.** Off by default (local demo). With `--auth` / `ECHOS_AUTH=on` three roles (caregiver < clinician < operator) log in with bearer tokens read from the environment; tokens live in sessionStorage in the browser, travel in the clear over HTTP (the API is bound to 127.0.0.1; there is no TLS) and the event stream takes its token in the URL. There are no user accounts, no password reset, no audit log beyond who acknowledged an alert, and the login rate limit is per process and in memory. Devices use a separate shared `X-Echos-Token` on the ingestion endpoint, which is unset (open) by default.
- Triage is a fixed if-then rule set; there is no trained fusion model (acknowledgement and escalation of alerts are handled by the Care Coordinator).
- The system does not forecast deterioration; it flags same-day differences from the person's own usual.
